"You're 29. When are you getting married?"

In some Chinese families, this isn't small talk. It's a question relatives ask at dinner — especially around New Year.

It's called 催婚 — cuīhūn — "urging marriage."

Many parents worry about stability, grandchildren, and what other people think. For many parents, marriage is not just romance. It's a safety net.

In smaller cities and rural areas, the pressure can be stronger.

But China is changing. Many young people are marrying later, or not at all. The question is still there. The answer is no longer automatic.

The dinner table is where it shows: one question, three generations, and a bowl of soup going cold.

Does your family ask you this? And what do you say?
