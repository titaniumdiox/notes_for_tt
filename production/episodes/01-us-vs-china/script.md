## 英文旁白全文

In the US, calling your friend's mom by her first name is normal.

In China, many people would use a kinship word instead — like “auntie” — even if she's not a relative.

It's not about being formal. It's about marking the relationship.

In the US, you open a gift right away. It shows you're excited.

In many Chinese homes, it can feel more polite to thank the person first — and open it later.

In the US, leaving a tip is expected.

In China, most restaurants don't expect one. The menu price is usually the price.

Different rules. Same everyday moment.

Which one would surprise you most?

## 屏幕字/标题备选

- 3 Things Normal in the US — Surprising in China
- “Auntie,” not “Linda”
- The gift you don't open right away
- Why no tip?

## 事实与表达边界

- 亲属称呼因地区、关系远近而异；“auntie/阿姨”只是近似翻译，不要暗示全国统一。
- 拆礼物习惯因家庭/地区而异；用 “many Chinese homes / can feel”，不要说 “Chinese people never open gifts.”
- 小费在旅游区、高端餐厅可能越来越常见；说 “most everyday restaurants”，不要说 “China has no tipping.”
- 不要拍成“美国正确/中国奇怪”；结尾用 “different rules”，不是 “weird”.

## 制作备注

- 竖屏优先上下分屏，避免左右分屏导致人物太小。
- 中文只做关键词卡，不做逐句教学。
- 结尾 3 秒留评论区提问，屏幕字不要挡住字幕安全区。
