At 2 p.m., a Chinese office can suddenly go quiet.

Lights off. Chairs back. Someone pulls out a folding bed.

This is 午休 — wǔxiū — the lunch-break nap.

In many Chinese schools and offices, the break is long enough for a short sleep — usually twenty to thirty minutes.

It's not laziness. Many people start early, commute far, and work late. A nap is a reset. The office doesn't stop. It just pauses.

In some schools, students sleep at their desks. In some offices, folding beds live under the desk.

Not everyone naps. But at the right time, the whole room can be asleep.
